\section{Analysis}\label{sec:task2-analysis}

This analysis is based on 18 real JPEG images, the official result file, and the corresponding run. Conclusions are limited to the current \(5712\times4284\) images, \(8\times5\) checkerboard inner corners, and 3 cm square size; a pixel error is not treated as a direct guarantee of physical measurement accuracy.

\subsection{Intrinsic-Parameter Consistency}

The estimated \(f_x\), \(f_y\), \(c_x\), and \(c_y\) are finite and positive. From the measured focal lengths,
\[
\rho_f=\frac{\lvert f_x-f_y\rvert}{(f_x+f_y)/2}
=0.0003511422\quad(0.035114\%).
\]
The two focal-length estimates are therefore very close, which is compatible with nearly square pixel scaling in this image set. It does not identify the exact rear-lens focal length or rule out image cropping. The image center is
\((W/2,H/2)=(2856,2142)\), while the principal-point offset is
\[
\left(c_x-W/2,c_y-H/2\right)
=(27.66418,-34.79706)\ {\rm px}.
\]
Its Euclidean length is \(44.45382\) px, approximately \(0.7783\%\) of the image width and \(1.0377\%\) of its height. The principal point is therefore near, but not identical to, the image center.

\subsection{Distortion and Error}

In the OpenCV order, the distortion vector is
\[
(0.20245123,-0.29420837,-0.00326679,0.00308671,-1.04554325).
\]
The radial and tangential correction terms are nonzero in this model. Their signs and magnitudes alone do not prove correction quality; the corrected image in Figure~\ref{fig:task2-visual-results} should be examined together with checkerboard edges in the original image.

The three error values are OpenCV RMS \(2.06424401\) px, mean pointwise reprojection error \(1.65696931\) px, and mean per-image RMS \(1.90750642\) px. They differ because the first aggregates squared point distances globally, the second averages point distances, and the third averages per-image RMS values. The per-image RMS range is \(0.850436\)--\(3.625463\) px; views with larger errors should be inspected if a refined acquisition is needed.

At the current resolution, the OpenCV RMS is approximately \(0.03614\%\) of the image width and \(0.04818\%\) of its height. Thus the error is small relative to the image dimensions, but a high-resolution image also makes a fixed pixel error appear smaller in relative terms. This metric is not an independent validation and should not be compared directly across resolutions without accounting for scale and acquisition conditions.

\subsection{Data Quality and Pose Coverage}

All 18 candidate JPEG files passed reading, \(8\times5\) corner detection, and the resolution check. The failure count is zero, and every image contributes 40 corners. The run therefore passes the program-level data-integrity checks. A separate pose log is not available in the current report, so the successful detection count alone cannot prove uniform coverage of the image center, edges, distances, and tilt angles. The original acquisition should be reviewed, especially for views with larger per-image RMS.

Because \texttt{square\_size=3.0} is in cm, the rotation vectors are dimensionless and the camera matrix and distortion coefficients remain pixel-coordinate quantities, while the translation vectors are measured in cm.
